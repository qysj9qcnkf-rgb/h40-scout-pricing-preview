How to Add Different FAQs to Each Shopify Product

Add different FAQs to each Shopify product with metafields or separate templates, check them before publishing, and write questions shoppers really ask.

Scout guides · 5 min read

Short answerStore product-specific FAQs in product metafields and render them in the product template, or use separate templates when only a few products differ. Write the questions from what shoppers actually ask before buying.

If editing one product's FAQ changes the same answer on every product, the FAQ probably lives in a shared template. Products can share a layout without sharing every answer. Here's how to set that up, and how to choose questions worth answering.

Metafields method

Start with metafields when every product needs the same kind of FAQ section, but with different answers.

A template controls the layout of the page, and editing it affects every product that uses it. (Shopify Help Center: templates) A product metafield stores information that belongs to one product. In a compatible theme, you connect a section or block to that metafield as a dynamic source, so each product page shows its own content in the same layout. (Shopify Help Center: displaying metafields on your online store)

A simple setup:

  1. Decide your FAQ fields. For example, three question-and-answer pairs per product, or a few fixed topics like "Fit," "Care" and "What's included."
  2. Create product metafield definitions in your Shopify admin for each field.
  3. Fill in the values for two test products with clearly different answers.
  4. Connect the fields to the matching section or block settings in your theme editor, using dynamic sources.
  5. Preview both products. The layout should stay the same while the answers change.

Work in a duplicate or unpublished copy of your theme while you set this up. Theme support and setting names vary, so if a block won't accept a dynamic source, check your theme's documentation before editing code.

Separate templates method

Separate product templates make sense when a few products need a genuinely different page layout, not just different answers.

For example, a large piece of furniture might need a detailed dimensions and delivery section that would look odd on a small accessory. You can create a second product template, add the FAQ content that group needs, and assign it to just those products.

Use this method sparingly:

  • Good fit: a small group of products with a different structure.
  • Poor fit: a new template for every product just to change a sentence. That quickly becomes hard to maintain, because every shared change has to be made in many places.

For example, a candle store sells single candles and gift bundles. Every single candle needs the same FAQ rows: burn time, scent notes, wick care. Those answers live in metafields, so one template serves them all. The bundles are different. Shoppers ask what's inside, whether they can swap a scent, and whether it ships gift-wrapped. That page needs a "What's in the box" section and a different FAQ order, so the bundles get their own template, with their own metafields for the answers.

You can combine the two: use a few templates for the main product types, and metafields for the answers within each type.

When to change the layout instead

Sometimes the right fix isn't an FAQ at all.

Not every answer belongs inside an accordion. If a fact could make a product unsuitable, such as a missing part, a compatibility limit or a size restriction, put it near the price and the buy button, where shoppers decide. The FAQ can hold the longer explanation.

Take a lamp sold without a bulb. "Bulb not included" should be visible before purchase, not hidden in question six. Details on which bulb to buy can sit lower on the page.

If your FAQ keeps growing because the page leaves basic questions open, that's a sign the main product description needs work. An FAQ supports the page. It shouldn't stand in for it.

Checks before publishing

Before you roll product-specific FAQs out across your catalog, test them:

  • Accuracy: Is every answer correct for this exact product and variant?
  • Empty fields: What happens when a product has no FAQ? An empty "Care instructions" accordion looks broken. Fill the field, or check whether your theme hides a block when its source is empty. If it doesn't, you'll need a tested theme change (or ask your theme developer).
  • Long and odd content: Test a long answer, a list and a missing field. A layout that works for one short line may break with more.
  • Mobile: Can shoppers find and read the answers comfortably on a phone, without the FAQ pushing the buy button out of view?
  • Consistency: Do the FAQ, your policies and your support team give the same answers?
  • Ownership: Who updates these answers when the product changes?

Roll out to a few products first, check them live, then expand.

Where the questions should come from: your shoppers

The setup is the easy part. The hard part is choosing the right questions.

It's easy to write an FAQ from the owner's point of view, answering things the store wants to say, not what shoppers actually wonder before buying. The best questions come from shoppers themselves:

  • Pre-purchase messages and support tickets: what do people ask before they order?
  • Reviews: what do buyers say they wish they'd known?
  • Returns: what reasons come up again and again?
  • Conversations with shoppers: ask recent buyers what nearly stopped them, and ask people who didn't buy what they couldn't find out.

Use the shopper's wording in the question itself. "Will this fit a standard-size desk?" is more useful than "Product dimensions." Leave out questions added only to make the section longer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have a different FAQ on each Shopify product?

Yes. Store each product's FAQ in product metafields and connect them to your product template, or use a separate template for products that need a different layout.

Why does editing one product's FAQ change all my products?

The FAQ text is probably stored in a shared template. Templates apply to every product that uses them. Move product-specific answers into metafields.

Do I need an app to add product-specific FAQs?

Often not. Metafields and dynamic sources are built into Shopify, but support depends on your theme. Check your theme's documentation first.

What questions should go in a product FAQ?

The ones shoppers actually ask before buying. Pull them from support messages, reviews, returns and conversations with shoppers.
